# Anime opening: generate, then edit

**Lineage:** [Kōda / @aimikoda](https://x.com/aimikoda), attached transcription
supplied by the author, labelled “Seedance 2.5 Prompt” and “Opus Prompt”. The attachment
contains [this post link](https://x.com/aimikoda/status/2110120443390709934);
its connection to the complete text is unverified. See [source status](README.md).
Status: adapted workflow reference, not a tested model route or runnable template.

## Generation brief structure

Assign each reference a role: one carries face/identity and a distinctive hand
effect; another carries costume, body, pose, setting and painted effect shapes.
Describe a painterly action sequence with broad textured planes, a cool blue-teal
setting and warm coloured effects. Keep subject, costume and effect language
consistent across shots. Make screen direction and camera-axis continuity explicit.

The supplied example is a vertical 30-second solo martial-arts sequence in four
scenes and 16 shots. It moves from stillness, through a first form and a quiet
pause, into an escalating final strike and a held pose. It reserves one brief
speed change for the final impact. Its generation brief excludes subtitles,
music, opponents and weapons; it favours painted effects over glossy CG fire.

## Separate finishing brief

Edit the selected footage as an anime-opening sequence: use deliberate cuts,
coherent grading, impact accents, transitions and graphic layers to support its
rhythm. Preserve a quiet section so every moment is not equally loud. Keep the
character readable and match physical sound accents to contact frames.

Reference exclusions belong to their stage: “no background music” in the
generation pass is compatible with adding a chosen soundtrack during the edit.
If a different result needs a song, quote/approve that generation separately.

Scrolls can hold reference roles and shot intentions. Existing Director grade,
shake, transition, speed-line and title modules can support the edit; they do
not guarantee action choreography or identity continuity. Adapt the generation
prompt to the chosen model's documented reference limits and prompting guide.
